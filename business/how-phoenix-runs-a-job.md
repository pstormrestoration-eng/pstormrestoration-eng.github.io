# How Phoenix runs a job

How [Phoenix Storm Restoration](phoenix-storm-restoration.md) handles a roof project.

## Five steps
1. **Inspection:** a drone and ladder inspection, with a high-resolution photo report sent by email.
2. **Consultation:** the team reviews the findings, talks through next steps and explains the claim process.
3. **Project signing:** scope, schedule, material and shingle color choices, and insurance and payment details are settled before work starts.
4. **Installation:** an on-site project supervisor is there from start to finish.
5. **Quality control:** a final inspection checks workmanship against manufacturer specs and local code, and the property is cleaned.

## Install day
- Crews usually arrive between 6:30 and 7:30 am.
- Homeowners don't have to be home but should be reachable by phone. If there's a gas line, the crew arranges attic access.
- Landscaping is tarped, A/C units are covered, and windows and patios are protected. The crew walks the property before and after.
- It's loud, which matters for people working from home or with pets or young kids.
- If rain is likely within 24 hours, the job is rescheduled.
- Cleanup includes a magnetic nail sweep and debris removal. A supervisor or project manager walks the job with the homeowner at the end.

## Payment
- **Retail jobs:** a deposit at signing and the balance at completion.
- **Insurance jobs:** paid in stages as the insurer releases funds. The first check is the actual cash value (ACV): the roof's value minus depreciation. The withheld depreciation is usually released 7 to 14 business days after the insurer gets the completion documents (final invoice, photos and any supplements).
- The homeowner's deductible is typically collected at signing or before completion. The insurer pays the homeowner, and the homeowner pays Phoenix; Phoenix does not bill the insurer directly.
- No money changes hands until the project starts, materials are delivered or supplementing begins.
